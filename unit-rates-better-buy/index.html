Worked example

When it doesn't divide evenly

$11.00 · 3 candy apples
11.00 ÷ 3 = 3.666666…
3.667 — the decider is a 6, so round up
The calculator keeps going forever. We stop at three digits so every answer in the class looks the same.
Why three digits?
At cents, $3.67 and $3.67 look like a tie. At thousandths, 3.667 and 3.665 don't.

Turn and talk · 90 seconds

Spot the mistake

Jordan's whiteboard
Pizza: $9.00 for 4 slices
4 ÷ 9.00 = 0.444
"44 cents a slice — what a deal!"
Jordan's arithmetic is correct. The answer is still wrong. Why?
The fix

The units came out upside down

4 ÷ 9.00 = 0.444
slices per dollar
You get 0.444 of a slice for each dollar. True, but useless.
9.00 ÷ 4 = 2.250
dollars per slice
$2.25 a slice. That's the number you compare.
Say the units out loud. They'll catch the flip for you.

Think it through

Cheaper isn't always the answer

01
Will you use all of it?
A cheap 40-ounce popcorn is expensive if half of it hits the floor.
02
Is it the same thing?
Unit rates only compare fairly when the item is the same.
03
Can you afford it today?
The better unit rate can still be a bigger total at the register.
The unit rate finds the better price. You decide the better buy.
